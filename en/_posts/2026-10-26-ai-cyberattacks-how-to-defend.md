The topic now appears in every security story: from the Tgcom24 interview with Clusit president Anna Vaccarelli to the debates over unfiltered AI models on Hugging Face. Artificial intelligence is changing the way attacks are carried out. The question for a business is: what changes for me?

## What is changing
Vaccarelli explained that cybercriminals absorb new technology **faster** than defenders: they started using AI first, including to write malware. They are organized like real companies, with roles and recruitment, and even sell **ready-made attack kits**. Law enforcement uses these tools too, but with a small delay.

The PHP2Shell case shows the practical effect: as Wiz researchers explained, flaws that had existed for decades were hard to turn into attacks, but the most advanced models have lowered that barrier. See [the dedicated article]({{ '/en/blog/' | relative_url }}).

## What does NOT change
Here is the good news. For most successful attacks, the causes are the same as ever:
- **out-of-date software**, with flaws already known and fixes already available;
- **weak, reused or carelessly given-away credentials**;
- **forgotten access** and systems nobody checks any more;
- **no plan and no tested backups**.

AI makes it faster to find and exploit these weak points, but it does not create new ones. So the basic defenses remain the most effective, but the time available is shrinking: fewer and fewer days pass between an advisory and the first attack.

## Five defenses that work
1. **Update early**: PHP, CMS, plugins, device firmware.
2. **Reduce what is exposed**: close panels, ports and services you do not need.
3. **Strong credentials and two-step verification**, not shared between people.
4. **Check**: new files, unusual access, strange requests in the logs.
5. **Backups with a restore test** and a written restart procedure.
