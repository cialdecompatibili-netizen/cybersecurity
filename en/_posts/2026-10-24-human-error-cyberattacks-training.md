We picture the hacker "breaking into" a system with movie tricks. In reality, according to Anna Vaccarelli, president of Clusit, in 95% of cases the attack succeeds because of **human error**. She said so on Tgcom24 on 26 October 2026, pointing to training, culture and awareness as the first remedy.

## How an attack that starts with a person works
An example from the interview: you work on a computer in the office and part of the software is out of date. The criminals scan the network, find that gap and send a **social engineering** message asking for credentials. If, distracted, we hand them over, they get into our computer and from there into the rest of the system. One employee's machine becomes the bridge to the whole company.

The messages change shape: the fake email from the bank, the "grandchild" asking for money, the courier that cannot deliver. The pattern is the same: urgency, authority, a link or a request for data.

## Habits that work
- **Distrust and verify.** If a message asks for money, data or passwords, check another way: call the bank or the person on a number you already know.
- **Never give credentials by email, chat or phone.** No serious service asks for them like that.
- **A different password for every service**, with two-step verification wherever it is available.
- **Update immediately**: computers, phones, website, plugins. A flaw that has been fixed is of no use to an attacker.
- **Report without shame.** Anyone who has clicked a wrong link must be able to say so at once, without fear of blame: every hour counts.

## What the owner can do
1. Talk about it with your people, with short, real examples, from time to time.
2. Separate access: everyone has their own credentials, with only the permissions they need.
3. Remove access when someone leaves the company or changes role.
4. Have a clear contact to write or phone in case of doubt.

Training does not eliminate the risk, but it reduces it a lot and costs little compared with an incident. The technical part is ours: updated systems, controlled access, verified backups.
