"We are small, who would be interested in us?" It is the sentence we hear most often and the most dangerous. In an interview with Tgcom24 published on 26 October 2026, Anna Vaccarelli, president of Clusit (the Italian Association for Information Security), explained that hackers do not target only banks and large groups: ordinary citizens and companies of every size are in their sights too.

## What they are really after
The reason, according to Vaccarelli, is almost always financial: the vast majority of attacks come from criminals who want to make money, while attacks made to send a message are a minority. And what has value is **data**: a customer list with names and addresses, payment details, health information. If you have a website with a contact form, management software or an online shop, you hold that data too.

There is also a second use, less obvious: your computer or your website can become a **bridge** to someone else. A poorly protected supplier is the way in to the large customer.

## Why a small business is a convenient target
- **Out-of-date software.** A known flaw that was never fixed is an invitation. That is why advisories like the one on [PHP2Shell]({{ '/en/blog/' | relative_url }}) matter even to those with "just" a website.
- **Nobody in charge.** In many SMEs security belongs to nobody: an outside contractor built the site years ago and no one checks it any more.
- **Few controls.** No logs being watched, no tested backups, no written procedures.

Vaccarelli notes that small and medium-sized businesses often lack precisely the culture of security and the resources to deal with it. The hospitality sector, she adds, is among the hardest hit.

## Where to start
1. **List what you have online**: websites, applications, servers, mailboxes, panels.
2. **Check the versions** of PHP, your CMS and plugins, and update.
3. **Change weak or old passwords** and turn on two-step verification where possible.
4. **Check your backups**: do they exist, are they recent, can they be restored?
5. **Write down who to call** if something goes wrong.
